## Research (current)

AI agents are becoming real economic actors, not just tools. Visa and
Mastercard have both launched agent-initiated payment rails (Intelligent
Commerce, Agent Pay), OpenAI's Agentic Commerce Protocol lets ChatGPT
complete purchases directly, and Amazon's Rufus now buys on a shopper's
behalf. That same shift is underway in financial markets: institutions
are moving from rule-based algorithmic trading toward LLM-based
multi-agent systems that synthesize signals, draft orders, and manage
risk within defined limits, a pattern researchers describe as "bounded
autonomy" trading. My question: when decision authority shifts from
humans to AI agents like these, do the classical guarantees markets are
designed around (strategy-proofness, efficiency, credibility) still hold,
or does market design need to be rebuilt for agents as participants?

- **agent-market-harness** — Go/Python harness implementing canonical
  auction mechanisms (second-price, first-price, English) with scripted
  and LLM-based agents, testing whether classical design guarantees hold
  under LLM bidders. Working paper in progress; private for now, will
  open up once results are ready to share.
- **Working paper (planned):** Prompts as incentive contracts: delegation
  to market agents.
